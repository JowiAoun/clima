The statute

  17 U.S.C. § 105 — Subject matter of copyright: United States Government works

  Copyright protection under this title is not available for any work of the
  United States Government, but the United States Government is not precluded
  from receiving and holding copyrights transferred to it by assignment,
  bequest, or otherwise.

  § 101 defines a "work of the United States Government" as a work prepared by
  an officer or employee of the United States Government as part of that
  person's official duties.


What it covers here

The severe-weather alert responses recorded from api.weather.gov, listed in
REUSE.toml. They are produced by the National Weather Service, an agency of the
National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration within the United States
Department of Commerce, as part of its statutory duties.


Two limits worth stating

Within the United States these works are in the public domain. Some other
jurisdictions do not recognise a government-work exception, and NOAA's own
disclaimer notes that material on its sites may occasionally include work owned
by third parties, which this exception does not reach.

Neither limit affects Clima's use: the recordings are unmodified responses used
to test a parser, and the National Weather Service is credited in the
application through NwsAlertProvider::attribution() whether or not any licence
compels it.
